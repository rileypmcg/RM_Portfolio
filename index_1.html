AI smart glasses

Hardware lead, Real World Engineering Club, 2025 to present. Four-person team.

Wearable glasses that capture audio and video, stream both over Wi-Fi to a server, and play back responses through an onboard speaker as real-time commands come in. I own the hardware: architecture, schematic, layout, and bring-up.

The prototype

The first version was built from an ESP32-S3 dev board and off-the-shelf modules. I brought it up and debugged it to VGA video at 10 FPS with 100 ms end-to-end latency, running on a single 1000 mAh Li-ion cell. It proved the system worked and showed exactly where it didn't: too big to wear, and too slow to feel live.

The custom board

I'm now designing a 4-layer PCB in Altium that puts the ESP32-S3, the camera interface, the analog audio path, and the regulated power rails on one board. It integrates 124 components and cuts board area by 70% against the dev-board prototype.

RV32I processor core In progress: specification

Solo project, 2026 to present. SystemVerilog.

A 5-stage pipelined RISC-V core built from scratch. I'm currently specifying the base integer instruction subset, where the pipeline stage boundaries fall, and how the core resolves data hazards: forwarding from the EX/MEM and MEM/WB registers, and a stall for load-use cases that forwarding can't cover.

No RTL is written yet. This page shows the plan, not a finished core.

Planned next

Write the RTL, verify it against the official riscv-tests compliance suite, then synthesize it onto an FPGA.
